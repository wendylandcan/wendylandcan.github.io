About me

Why I chose AI

Hi! I’m Wendy, an ENFP through and through and a small-town kid who got here by studying hard. I spent seven years studying law. With graduation coming up, I decided to switch careers and go all in on AI.

An idea used to get stuck because you couldn’t code or design it yourself. Now AI can help you make a first version and show it to someone. That’s what excites me. So many more people get to make things and share their ideas. I want to see where that takes us and work with other people on things someone could actually use.

I believe we’re heading into a human-centered renaissance. As we get better at making things, I keep coming back to a few questions. What’s worth doing? What kind of life do we want? Who do we want to create with?

I believe it’s more important to build for the public than to build in public. I want to build things that help people find each other and give each other room to talk. If that sounds like your kind of thing, come say hi if we cross paths!

Enterprise AI

AI Product Intern

NIO Legal AI Platform

One place to ask a legal question, look up guidance, and follow a case with your coworkers.

Project details · NIO Legal AI Platform
Why we built it
People had to figure out which channel to use and who to ask. Earlier answers were hard to find, so the same questions kept coming up.
What I did
I helped build the platform and designed how it handled questions, cases, law firms, guidance, and updates. In Feishu, the agent can look up an answer, hand off to the legal team when needed, and keep a record of what happened.
How we tested it
We replayed past conversations and asked legal staff to spot-check whether answers had sources and whether the agent asked for missing details. We also pulled together examples to retest after changes.

Labor Law

Product Lead

JobPilot

A tool for workers, employers, and lawyers. It asks what happened first, then explains its analysis and shows its sources.

Project details · JobPilot
Why we built it
Was a contract signed? When did the person leave? Where did they work? Missing one detail can change the answer to a labor-law question.
What I did
I mapped 24 scenarios and designed the agent to identify the kind of question, ask for missing facts, and then explain the legal analysis. Workers, employers, and lawyers get reports tailored to them. The agent looks up laws and cases through MCP and uses a rules-based calculator for compensation.
What came of it
We placed second out of 173 teams in the law firm track of the second iCourt National Legal AI Competition.

Independent Project

Design & Development

Liqingai

A friend asked me to settle a disagreement, so I made a little online courtroom where both people get to tell their side.

Project details · Liqingai
Why I built it
When you ask AI who was right after an argument, it usually hears one person’s version. I wanted the other person to have a place to say how they felt and fill in anything that was missing.
What I did
I borrowed the format from court. Both people can give their account, share screenshots or audio, and respond to each other. They see the same session as it happens, and the AI writes a “verdict” at the end.
What came of it
I split tasks between larger and smaller models and cached results I could reuse. API costs for complex cases fell by more than 60%.

Project details · Order Review Cat
Why I built it
The legal team spent a lot of time checking orders against spreadsheets. After enough trips back and forth, small details were easy to miss.
